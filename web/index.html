Your home is still in your photos.

After a fire, flood or earthquake, the insurer asks for a list of everything you owned: what it was, how old, what it costs to replace. Most families have no list. But the birthday photos, the Christmas mornings and the first-day-of-school pictures show the rooms behind the people. Roomprint reads them, prices each thing at a real store, and hands you a list your insurer can load, with proof on every line.

The sample family, their home and photos are made up. Your own photos are read once to build your list and are not used for anything else.

    How it works

    1. Read each photo. NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super VL looks past the people at the room: the TV, the sofa, the stand mixer, the bikes on the garage wall. It gives each thing a box on the photo, and that crop becomes the line's evidence. Code drops people, food and party decorations, and moves the house itself (cabinets, sinks, flooring) to a separate list, because policies pay for those under the building, not the contents.
    2. Count each thing once. The same TV is in the birthday photo and the Christmas photo. Code only pairs sightings that could be the same thing (same room, same kind of item, no clashing brand); Nemotron VL then looks at the two crops side by side. Two lamps in one photo are always two lamps.
    3. Price it at a real store. Tavily searches for a new like-kind replacement. Code keeps only sentences that name the item next to a dollar amount, Nemotron 3 Super picks the fair ones, and code checks every pick: the amount is in that sentence, it isn't used, "was", "starting at" or per-month. Two stores or more give the median; one store is flagged; none means "needs a price". Nothing is guessed.
    4. Show the maths. Age comes from the earliest photo the thing appears in. Actual cash value follows the depreciation rules State Farm publishes (effective age by condition, capped at 80%) with the useful lives from United Policyholders, and every line shows its own calculation.
    5. Hand it over in their columns. One click gives State Farm's XactContents worksheet or FEMA's contents worksheet, with the store link in each line.
